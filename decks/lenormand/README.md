# Petit Lenormand

**36-card French cartomancy system from early 1800s**

## Overview

The Petit Lenormand is named after the famous French fortune teller Marie Anne Lenormand (1772-1843), though the system itself derives from the German "Game of Hope" card game. It became one of the most popular cartomancy methods in 19th century Europe and remains widely used today.

## Card Structure

Each of the 36 cards combines three elements:

1. **Sequential number** (1-36)
2. **Symbolic image** (Rider, Clover, Ship, etc.)
3. **Playing card equivalent** (suit and rank)

This triple correspondence allows for multiple layers of interpretation.

## How to Use

### Reading Methods

**Single Card** - Quick daily guidance
**Three Cards** - Situation/Action/Outcome or Past/Present/Future  
**Line of Five** - Detailed situation analysis
**Nine-Card Square** - Comprehensive reading
**Grand Tableau** - All 36 cards for complete life overview

### Combination Reading

Lenormand's power lies in combining cards:
- **Ship + Clover** = Lucky journey
- **Snake + Ring** = Troubled relationship
- **Key + Fish** = Financial solution
- **Mountain + Rider** = Delayed news

Cards modify each other, creating nuanced meanings.

### Distance Reading

In spreads, cards close to the significator (Man/Woman) are immediate and important. Distant cards represent background influences or future events.

### Mirroring and Knighting

Advanced techniques include:
- **Mirroring** - Cards equidistant from significator
- **Knighting** - Cards that "jump" in knight's move pattern
- **Houses** - Card's position adds meaning (Grand Tableau)

## Playing Card Correspondences

The deck includes playing card suits:

**Hearts** - Love, emotions, relationships, happiness  
**Diamonds** - Money, practical matters, material world  
**Clubs** - Work, activity, communication, conflict  
**Spades** - Challenges, difficulties, lessons, endings

When a Lenormand card falls with its playing card showing, the suit can add nuance to the interpretation.

## Tips for Beginners

1. **Learn card meanings first** - Master individual cards
2. **Practice pairs** - Combine two cards daily
3. **Read literally** - Lenormand is concrete and practical
4. **Keep it simple** - Direct interpretations work best
5. **Trust combinations** - Cards modify each other

## Traditions

### Historical Context
- Developed in early 19th century Germany
- Popularized in France by Mlle Lenormand
- Peak popularity: 1850-1950
- Revival in modern cartomancy

### Modern Practice
- Popular in Europe, especially Germany and France
- Growing English-speaking community
- Used for practical, everyday questions
- Known for specific, detailed answers

## No Reversals

Traditional Lenormand does not use reversed cards. All meanings come from:
- The card itself
- Combinations with neighbors
- Position in spread
- Distance from significator

## Sources

Based on the historical "Game of Hope" (Das Spiel der Hoffnung) and the practice of Mlle Lenormand. Card meanings have been standardized through centuries of use.
