# Playing Card Cartomancy

**52-card standard deck divination from medieval Europe**

## Overview

Playing card divination (cartomancy) dates back to 14th century Europe and became especially popular in the 18th-19th centuries. It uses a standard 52-card deck for straightforward, practical readings.

## The Four Suits

**Hearts ♥️ - Love and Emotions**
- Relationships, home life, feelings
- Joy, happiness, emotional fulfillment
- Family, friends, romance

**Diamonds ♦️ - Money and Resources**
- Financial matters, work, career
- Material success, business ventures
- Resources, practical concerns

**Clubs ♣️ - Passion and Action**
- Creativity, inspiration, energy
- Work, activity, accomplishment
- Ideas, communication, conflicts

**Spades ♠️ - Challenges and Truth**
- Difficulties, obstacles, warnings
- Endings, change, transformation  
- Hard truths, necessary lessons

## Card Values

**Aces** - Beginnings, opportunities, gifts  
**2-10** - Increasing intensity of suit energy  
**Jacks** - Young people, messages, energy  
**Queens** - Mature women, nurturing, wisdom  
**Kings** - Mature men, authority, mastery

## How to Use

### Recommended Spreads
- **Single Card** - Yes/no or daily guidance
- **Three Cards** - Quick situation reading
- **Five Cards** - Week ahead or decision-making
- **Celtic Cross** - Comprehensive life reading

### Reading Style

Playing cards provide **direct, practical answers**:
- More literal than tarot
- Focused on everyday situations
- Good for specific questions
- Clear yes/no guidance

### No Reversals

Traditional cartomancy doesn't use reversed cards. Suit and rank provide the meaning.

## Court Card Associations

Courts can represent people or energies:

**Jacks** - Youth, messengers, new energy  
**Queens** - Feminine energy, nurturing, receptive  
**Kings** - Masculine energy, authority, action

Suits indicate temperament:
- Hearts: Emotional, loving
- Diamonds: Practical, ambitious
- Clubs: Creative, active
- Spades: Serious, wise

## Sources

Based on centuries of European cartomancy tradition. Methods evolved organically through folk practice and have been documented since the 1700s.
