# I Ching (Book of Changes)

**64-hexagram ancient Chinese oracle, 3000+ years old**

## Overview

The I Ching (易經, Yìjīng) is one of the oldest divination systems in the world, dating back over 3,000 years to ancient China. It serves as both an oracle and a philosophical text, offering guidance on life's situations and changes.

## Structure

### Hexagrams
Each of the 64 hexagrams consists of **six horizontal lines**, either:
- **Broken (yin)** ⚋ - Receptive, feminine, dark
- **Unbroken (yang)** ⚊ - Creative, masculine, light

### Trigrams
The six lines form two **trigrams** (three-line patterns):
- Heaven (☰), Earth (☷), Thunder (☳), Water (☵)
- Mountain (☶), Wind (☴), Fire (☲), Lake (☱)

## The 64 Hexagrams

Each hexagram represents a unique life situation:

**1. Qian** - The Creative (Heaven)  
**2. Kun** - The Receptive (Earth)  
**3. Zhun** - Difficulty at Beginning  
**4. Meng** - Youthful Folly  
**5. Xu** - Waiting  
**6. Song** - Conflict  
... and 58 more, each with specific wisdom

## Traditional Consultation Methods

### Coin Method (Modern)
1. Toss three coins six times
2. Heads = yang (3), Tails = yin (2)
3. Sum determines line type
4. Build hexagram from bottom up

### Yarrow Stalks (Traditional)
Complex 50-stalk dividing method taking 10-15 minutes per reading.

### Random Selection (Plugin)
Digital method providing instant hexagram selection.

## How to Read

### The Judgment
Each hexagram begins with a brief statement about the situation.

### The Image
Poetic description using natural imagery to illustrate the hexagram's meaning.

### The Lines
Six line texts (爻辭) offering specific guidance. Changing lines point to a second hexagram showing how the situation will evolve.

### Example Reading Process
1. Form your question clearly
2. Consult the oracle (draw hexagram)
3. Read the Judgment and Image  
4. Consider changing lines (if applicable)
5. Study second hexagram (if changes occur)
6. Meditate on the wisdom

## Key Concepts

**Wu Wei** - Non-action, going with the flow  
**Yin and Yang** - Complementary opposites  
**Change** - Constant transformation  
**Timing** - Right action at the right moment  
**Balance** - Harmony between extremes

## Tips for Beginners

1. **Keep questions open** - "What should I know about..." rather than "Should I..."
2. **Read slowly** - I Ching rewards contemplation
3. **Journal readings** - Meanings often unfold over time
4. **Study hexagrams** - Each is a deep teaching
5. **Trust the process** - Wisdom may not be immediate
